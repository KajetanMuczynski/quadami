# Quadami 0.3.0 release notes

The hand-written part of the 0.3.0 release notes; `ci/release.py` puts it into
[TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md). A `v0.3.0` tag fails while this file holds a placeholder (`ci/release.py` looks for the word). Each claim here must be
true of the tagged commit: update it in the release PR (docs/RELEASING.md, step 3).

## What changed

A feature release on top of 0.2.1: guide loops, a density preview, sharper hard edges and cone
tips, fewer broken perforated and lace-like parts, and faster runs at high counts. Like 0.2 it is a
release with known limits, not a stable one. Results differ from 0.2.1 on many models, mostly on
hard-surface parts, cones, perforated plates and lace. The numbers below are from our test sets,
each run 6 times with tiny changes to the input, and compared with the code before the change.

New in the panel:

- **Draw Guide**: draw a loop on the model (click or drag in Object Mode) and the quads follow it.
  **Mark Guide**, **Clear Guide** and **Clear Guides** work on selected edges. The result box shows
  how many guide loops there were and how many guide edges the quads followed. On our guide test
  set, 60 of 84 guide loops come out as closed edge loops (36 without guides).
  Guides do not make edge loops close by themselves elsewhere (see *Known limits*).
- **Preview Density**: shows the planned quad size on your model before you remesh (orange =
  smaller quads, blue = larger), with a **Change** control that moves Target Quads by 20 % steps.
  It takes about half the time of a Remesh; Esc cancels it.
- **Try N Target Quads**: when parts are lost, the panel suggests the next Target Quads with a
  **Set N** button, and after a rerun says how many of the lost places came back.
- **Faster repeated runs on the same mesh**: the prepared input is kept between runs (up to the 4
  newest meshes, at most 1.5 GB, in the add-on's own folder). A second Remesh or Preview of a
  large unchanged mesh is faster (a 15.5 MB tentacle model: 12.0 s to 5.9 s); below about 200,000
  triangles the gain is small.

Better results:

- **Hard edges and corners are sharper.** Creases of the input now survive the first remesh step,
  and the vertices on a crease slide along it while the quads are smoothed. On our Blender
  primitives, crease edges followed went from 65 % to 98 % and the largest distance from the input
  dropped by more than half; folded quads on those primitives went from 12 to 0.
- **Fewer folded quads next to creases**: of the two ways to finish the quads at a crease, the
  one with fewer folds on the input is kept. Folded faces went down on every test set, by 1 to a few
  faces per run (on our corpus set 51 to 48, no run worse).
- **Cone tips keep their point** instead of being rounded off (219 of 219 cone runs, none before).
- **Parts where sharp edges broke the shape** (holes or handles lost) are remeshed again without
  those edges; on our CAD set no run loses a hole or a handle any more. A warning says on how many
  parts the sharp edges were not kept.
- **Perforated plates, lace and woven parts tear far less.** Parts with many holes or handles are
  remeshed on a closed shell around them, and small crowded hole rims are left out before the
  remesh. On a plate with 8 × 8 small holes at 2,500 quads, the missing surface went from 4.1 % to
  under 0.01 %, and the plate no longer collapses into a ball.
- **No more crash on faces that use a vertex twice** (an OBJ line like `f 1 1 2`); such faces are
  removed and counted in the Input cleanup row.
- **No more crash or 25-minute stall at very high counts** (a head sculpt at 400,000 quads).

Faster:

- **Large runs are faster**: the clean-up after quad extraction no longer rebuilds everything on
  each pass (a head sculpt at 100,000 quads: 231-248 s to 136-143 s, same output), the rounding
  step keeps its work instead of starting over (4-35 % faster on our test models at 3,000 to
  24,000 quads), and very large solves use a better ordering (same output). The rounding and
  extraction changes move some vertices slightly but changed no measured quality number.

## Warnings in the panel

- **Missing parts** now starts with the share of the surface that is missing and turns red at 10 %
  or more; a new warning says "About N % of the input surface is missing from the result".
- **Sharp edges were not kept on N parts**: those parts were remeshed without their sharp edges,
  because the edges broke the part's shape.
- **Some marked edges were not followed on N parts**: guide or marked edges were dropped where
  following them folded the quads.
- **Small parts given a minimum of quads**: many small loose parts each got a minimum of quads, so
  the total is well above Target Quads; a lower Target Quads does not shrink them (join or delete
  small parts: Edit Mode, P > By Loose Parts).
- **Parts remeshed on a closed shell**: a part with many holes or handles was remeshed on a shell
  around it; thin gaps may be filled.
- **Long runs**: after 30 seconds the panel shows how long the run has taken and that there is no
  time limit; after several minutes without progress it says the run may be stuck. Nothing is
  stopped for you: press Cancel or Esc.
- **Imported Sharp marks**: when a mark covers more than half of the edges, the warning now says how
  to clear it in Edit Mode.

The other warnings are as in 0.2.1 (user guide §6).

## Known limits

As in [0.2.1](https://github.com/KajetanMuczynski/quadami/releases/tag/v0.2.1) (thin parts, folded
sculpt input, holes, hard-surface edges, the cleanup, Follow Marks, copied data, modifiers baked in,
Keep Ridges, very many tiny thin pieces), and:

- **Edge loops on organic models still spiral** instead of closing, as with every automatic remesher
  we compared. Guides help where you draw them, but on a head sculpt only a few guide loops close.
- **Repair Folds stays off by default**: it is offered by its button after a run with folds, as in
  0.2. It sometimes makes the result worse.
- **The minimum of quads for small parts** is only a warning: on models with very many small loose
  parts the result can have far more quads than Target Quads.
- **Sharper creases cost something**: on some scans and sculpts the result is up to about 10 %
  farther from the input than with 0.2.1, a few cylinder and cone rims get a handful more flipped
  quads, and one of our own sculpts gets more folded quads.
- **Perforated plates at a low Target Quads** can lose holes or collapse unless each hole is
  about 5 quads around (on our test plates, 39 to 80 quads per hole). Raise Target Quads.
- **Lace and perforated parts** come out closed rather than torn: thin gaps may be filled, and on
  our woven test parts a quarter to a half of the result lies more than half a quad away from the
  input. These runs can take 1.3 to 3 times as long.
- **The input cache** uses up to 1.5 GB in the add-on's folder. It is removed with the add-on.
- **Against 0.2.1 on our test sets** (release check, corpus and closed sets, 6 runs per case on
  Linux, Windows and macOS, run 45127503289 on `f175150`): passed: no measure got worse on clearly
  more cases than it got better (lowest one-sided p = 0.33, before correction). Folded quads per
  1,000 faces dropped from about 0.22 to 0.11-0.13 (corpus) and from about 1.45 to 0.53-0.77
  (closed thin shapes). Some tori at 150 quads still miss the quad count by more than 10 %, as in
  0.2.1.
- **Tried by hand on Linux** (Blender 4.5, this release's CI build): TBD.
- **macOS is experimental and untested**: nobody has run this release on a real Mac, and the program
  is not notarized, so macOS may block it (user guide §2). Reports from Mac users are welcome.
- Version numbers name releases only. Test builds between releases keep this version; tell them
  apart by the build line.
