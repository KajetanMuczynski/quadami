# Quadami 0.2.0 release notes

The hand-written part of the 0.2.0 release notes; `ci/release.py` puts it into
[TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md). A `v0.2.0` tag fails while this file holds a placeholder (`ci/release.py` looks for the word). Each claim here must be
true of the tagged commit: update it in the release PR (docs/RELEASING.md, step 3).

## What changed

A release with known limits, like 0.1.0, not a stable one: read *Known limits* below before relying
on it. Your results change against 0.1.0 on most models (cleaner input, sharp edges, fewer crashes),
so a model remeshed with 0.1.0 does not come out the same. Compared with 0.1.0:

- **The result keeps the original's data.** Under **Keep from Original** (a collapsed section of the
  panel): materials, vertex groups, colour attributes and the **sharp**, **crease** and **seam**
  marks, on by default; the UV maps, off by default. The line "Copied from the original: …" lists
  what came across (user guide §3).
- **Messy input is cleaned up first.** Pieces that only touch along a seam are welded (Blender's
  glTF import, text converted to mesh, an Edge Split modifier), duplicate faces are removed, points
  where two parts of the surface share one vertex are split, and paper-thin sliver pieces with no
  area are left out. These used to give cracks, thousands of empty pieces, minutes of work or an
  out-of-memory crash; on our test meshes they now take seconds. The **Input cleanup** row says
  what was done; your object is never changed.
- **Edges of exactly 90°** (Blender's cube and cylinder) are now kept as hard edges at the default
  Sharp Edge of 90°. Before, rounding noise in the coordinates decided it, so a cube moved by a hair
  could lose most of its edges; you no longer need 89° for them.
- **Fewer hangs and crashes.** Some inputs made the quad extraction loop forever or crash (a
  mosquito model at 2,000 quads, a watch model, a lace model at 3,000 and 10,000 quads); the piece
  concerned now goes to the fallback extractor or fails on its own, and the rest of the model is
  remeshed.
- **Less memory**: the remesher's peak memory is lower without changing the result, by about 58 %
  on a model of 4,888 loose pieces, 23 % on a 2-million-triangle scan and 14 % on a 100,000-triangle
  sculpt at 50,000 quads.
- **Missing parts say why.** The warning now names the cause: parts thinner than one quad ("a limit
  of Target Quads, not a remeshing error"), separate pieces that got no quads, the edges of
  single-sided surfaces, or something else, with what raising Target Quads did on our test models.
- **Quad Smoothing** (Advanced, experimental): **Keep Ridges** slides the quads along the surface
  instead of moving them in 3D, so ridges, swirls and vines stay sharper. Off by default (Standard).
- The panel's text wraps to the sidebar's real width instead of being cut off.

## Warnings in the panel

New or changed since 0.1.0; the others are as in 0.1.0 (user guide §6).

- **Missing parts** now comes with one sentence per cause: *thin parts* ("a limit of Target Quads,
  not a remeshing error"; raise Target Quads, 1.5 to 8 times as many brought the parts lost first
  back on 7 of 10 test models, or thicken the part), *pieces with no quads*, *open edges of
  single-sided surfaces* (leaves, petals, cloth), and *other*, which asks for a report.
- **Parts thinner than one quad** now says it is a limit of Target Quads at the target you asked for.
- **Input cleanup** lists welded seams, removed duplicate faces, split pinched points and left-out
  sliver pieces.
- **"Could not copy data from the original: …"**: copying materials, groups, marks or UVs failed; the
  result itself is fine.

## Known limits

- **Thin parts** (leaves, vines, cloth, blade edges) thinner than about one quad can fold, lose
  pieces, or lose their handles (small tunnels close up). The panel says so; raise Target Quads or
  thicken the part. The remesher does not put smaller quads on thin parts (tried for this release
  and not adopted: it either acted like a higher Target Quads for the whole model or moved the
  losses elsewhere). Raising Target Quads need not clear the warning: a higher target measures the
  parts more finely and finds thinner ones.
- **Folded sculpt input** is where most remaining defects on our test sculpts sit. Fold repair is off
  by default and leaves spots it cannot fix safely; folds wider than about 1 % of the model are not
  detected.
- **Holes** can still appear in closed sculpts, mostly next to folded input and at very high Target
  Quads; the **Holes** row shows them. **Repair Folds and Re-run** closed them on our folded test
  head, but it flattens fine detail such as swirls.
- **Hard-surface models**: edges still come out slightly rounded, with rows of thin quads along them
  at times: on Blender's cube an edge loop now follows about 90 % of the edges at 5,000 quads (72 % at
  1,000), on the cylinder about 80 % of the rims (74 % at 1,000). A cone's base rim is mostly rounded
  off and its tip is cut off about one quad deep. **Edges marked Sharp in Blender are not used** to
  guide the quads (the marks are copied onto the result, see *What changed*). The 90° change costs a
  little elsewhere on our test sets: a box with a sculpted relief has about 12 folded quads
  instead of 4 at 5,000 quads, a rotated mechanical bracket loses a handle at 1,000 quads, a
  vine-covered sword more folded faces at 3,000 quads.
- **The cleanup can join what you meant to keep apart**: open borders that lie on top of each other
  within about 1/100,000 of the model's size are welded (two sheets with their own vertices lying on
  the same side stay apart). It cannot be switched off. Splitting pinched points can add a few folded
  quads (a plant model: about 86-89 instead of 80-83 at 3,000 quads), and welding gave a dragon model
  65-91 non-quads instead of 2 at 3,000 quads in half of our runs.
- **A very dense lace-like piece** can now come out with no quads (it is reported as an empty island)
  instead of the run taking many minutes or running out of memory.
- **Copied data**: materials, groups and marks follow the new quads, not your borders: a material
  border zigzags along the quads, a marked line becomes the nearest run of quad edges, and lines
  shorter than about one quad are dropped. **UV Maps** keeps each face inside one UV island, but on
  models with many small islands up to 6 % of the faces come out distorted. Shape keys and face sets
  are not kept.
- **Modifiers are baked in**: an enabled Mirror, Subdivision Surface or Armature is applied into the
  remesh and is not on the result.
- **Keep Ridges** quad smoothing can leave a few more quads folded over in deep grooves (a swirled
  cube: about 6 more out of 3,000), which is why it is not the default.
- **Thousands of tiny loose parts** (chain mail, scattered debris): every piece gets a minimum number
  of quads, so the result can have far more quads than the target, and some pieces still come out
  empty. Raising Target Quads does not help here; merge or delete the small parts first.
- **Openings in open parts** (gums, clothes) can close, open or change shape without the **Holes**
  row counting them; it counts only holes in closed parts.
- **Object scale**: a non-uniform scale stretches the quads; the panel warns, but apply the scale
  first (**Ctrl+A** > **Scale**). No mirror symmetry (remesh one half and add a Mirror modifier),
  guide curves or density painting yet.
- **Large inputs** are slow to hand over (the mesh goes through a text file both ways).
- **The same input gives slightly different results on Linux, Windows and macOS** (each is repeatable
  on its own).
- **Tried by hand** with this release's build: TBD
  Linux, Windows and macOS all pass the automatic tests in Blender without a window.
- **Windows**: the program is not signed, so SmartScreen may warn before it first runs.
- **macOS is experimental and untested**: nobody has run this release on a real Mac, and the program
  is not notarized, so macOS may block it (user guide §2). Reports from Mac users are welcome.
- Version numbers name releases only. Test builds between releases keep this version; tell them
  apart by the build line.
